Questions.

What can I use as collateral?

Native ETH deposited into a dedicated Rino-controlled Ethereum vault. The current fixed offer requires at least 0.1126 ETH, including a 0.0126 ETH settlement reserve, for one 100 CREDIT loan. These test terms use a fixed 2,000 CREDIT per ETH reference and 50% maximum LTV, rather than a live market price. External vault balances cannot be imported.

Does Rino support multiple positions?

The app lists configured, isolated markets. Each market has one vault and loan, with its own collateral, debt and maturity. Separate controllers issue separate CREDIT tokens, so balances remain market-specific. Creating a new market requires a separate reviewed deployment.

Why does the app use two wallets?

Your NEAR loan account signs borrowing, repayment and loan management. Your Ethereum collateral wallet sends ETH to your designated vault. Connect NEAR to begin; connect Ethereum when you add collateral. They remain separate accounts with separate approvals.

How do repayment and release work?

For the public test offer, repay the full 101 CREDIT on NEAR before maturity, then request ETH release to the designated borrower address. Ethereum completion must be authenticated on NEAR before closure. Fees come from the vault. Terminal vaults retire and must not receive new deposits.

What happens at maturity?

CREDIT repayment is unavailable at or after maturity. The lender can lock default settlement. Under the public test terms the lender’s ETH payout is capped at 0.0505 ETH, subject to available funds and fees. Remaining borrower surplus is returned separately.

Which networks does Rino use?

Rino uses Ethereum Sepolia and NEAR testnet. CREDIT is a test asset with no dollar peg or promised redemption. In public mode your connected wallets authorize financial actions. Configured RPC, MPC, executor services and developer full-access keys remain trusted; a completed local demonstration does not establish public-chain execution or TEE verification.
